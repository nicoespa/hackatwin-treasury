# Proposed policy vs a fixed buffer set ahead of time

Generated by `scripts/exante_buffer_experiment.py data/sim/intl data/sim/base` (`exante_buffer.csv`).
Eight weekly origins ending 2026-10-31, 14-day horizon, 1000 paths, alpha 0.99.

The factorial tables match the fixed buffer on the evaluated weeks themselves (`fixed_hindsight`): the
fewest buffer days with the proposed policy's shortfall rate on those same weeks. No treasury can set
that number in advance. `fixed_exante` picks the fewest days with zero shortfalls over the 16 weeks
before the first evaluated origin and then runs them on the evaluated weeks. The proposed policy runs
on the simulator's segment labels (`proposed_oracle`) and on k-means segments inferred from the
transactions (`proposed_kmeans`), which is what a real wallet can do.

What it says:

- Against a buffer set ahead of time, the proposed policy holds less capital on every rail, with no
  shortfalls. On SWIFT abroad the calibration weeks include the July winter holidays, so the buffer
  that never failed there needs 10.75 days.
- Only a buffer tuned on the evaluated weeks beats it on SWIFT. That is a lower bound for any fixed
  rule, not a rule a treasury can run.
- Abroad, k-means segments do as well as the simulator's labels (600 k vs 637 k on SWIFT); at home
  they cost capital (see `segmentation.md`).
