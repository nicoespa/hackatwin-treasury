## Setup

The wallet is Argentine; it prefunds two partners abroad, each in its own currency:

- `pix_br` (BRL): pays Pix QR payments made by Argentine users in Brazil (`pix_brazil`), settled in real
  time, 24/7.
- `usd_acquirer` (USD): settles international card purchases (`intl_card`, net of `intl_card_refund`)
  T+1 on US business days, outbound remittances (`remittance_out`, batched with the cards: a
  simplification), and collects USD freelance income (`usd_income`), which nets against the balance.

Rails (`config/intl/rails.yaml`): `swift` buys USD and wires it, 2 business days counted only on days
that are business days in Argentina and in the partner's country, instruction cutoff 13:00, credited
at 12:00, USD 30 + 30 bps; `stablecoin` (USDT) lands in 30 minutes, 24/7, USD 1 + 40 bps. Every
partner accepts both by default; the variant `usd_acquirer swift only` runs the stablecoin rail with
the US partner on SWIFT (`ConfigSet` `accepts` override, or `accepts_rails: [swift]` in the YAML).

Calendars: `config/calendar_ar.yaml` (shared), `config/intl/calendar_br.yaml` (FEBRABAN national bank
holidays, Carnaval Monday and Tuesday included) and `config/intl/calendar_us.yaml` (Federal Reserve
Bank holidays; 2026-07-03 is open). A SWIFT order to `usd_acquirer` on Friday 2026-01-16 lands on
Wednesday 01-21 (MLK Monday); one to `pix_br` on Wednesday 2026-06-03 lands Monday 06-08 (Corpus
Christi).

FX (`config/intl/market.yaml`, `treasury.fx`): daily ARS/USD from 1450 with a 1.5 %/month crawl,
0.5 % daily noise and devaluation jumps (1 %/day probability, mean 4 %); BRL/USD from 5.38, no drift,
0.7 % daily noise; stored in `fx.parquet`. In this run ARS/USD ends at about 1840. Demand for
`pix_brazil` scales by `exp(-1.2 × log(ARS/BRL ÷ its previous-90-day mean))` and by a tourism-season
index (January 2.0, February 1.6, Carnaval 2.3, Semana Santa 1.6, July winter holidays 1.5, early
summer and long weekends 1.4); `intl_card` scales with elasticity 0.4 to ARS/USD's 30-day change.
The existing `FxJump` scenario (a crypto-buying scare) is domestic; here the jumps live in the FX path
and demand reacts through the elasticity.

Forecast drivers (`features.build_drivers`, read by `aggregate_lgbm.driver_features` and the segment
model): `fx_ars_per_brl_chg90` and `fx_ars_per_usd_chg30` (frozen at their last value before the
origin by `features.known_at`: a forecast never sees future rates), `tourism_index` (calendar, known
ahead) and `settles_usd_acquirer`, the calendar days of events settling on each date on the US
calendar (3 on a Tuesday, 4 after a Monday holiday, 0 on weekends and US holidays). The rate levels
are in the drivers table but not model features (they trend with the crawl).

Policy (`config/intl/policy.yaml`): alpha 0.99, lot 100 (partner currency), cost of capital **8 %/yr on
USD balances**, and `place_lagged_at_cutoff: true`: a lagged partner's outflow is placed at its 17:00
settlement instead of 00:00, so a SWIFT credit at 12:00 covers that day's T+1 settlement. The
domestic policy keeps the 00:00 placement.

USD conversion (`treasury.intl.usd_by_partner`): capital converted ledger row by row at the day's
rate and time-weighted; shortfalls at the period's mean rate; fees as USD fixed fee plus bps of the
amount converted at the instruction day's rate. `cost_total_usd` = 8 % × capital × 52/365 + fees +
0.05 per USD short.

## Results (USD, post-warm-up)

| rail | variant | fixed 5 days: capital / shortfall rate | proposed: capital / shortfall rate / fees | matched buffer: days / capital / fees | capital saved vs matched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| swift | all accept stablecoin | 350 k / 51.9 % | 637 k / 0 % / 13.9 k | 6.56 / 455 k / 14.9 k | **-40 %** (proposed uses more) |
| stablecoin | all accept stablecoin | 403 k / 0 % | 204 k / 0 % / 16.7 k | 4.44 / 357 k / 17.3 k | **43 %** |
| stablecoin | usd_acquirer swift only | 362 k / 25.0 % | 573 k / 0 % / 14.0 k | 6.56 / 469 k / 14.9 k | -22 % |

Per partner (proposed vs matched): on stablecoin `pix_br` holds BRL 131 k (USD 26 k) vs BRL 311 k
(USD 62 k) and `usd_acquirer` USD 178 k vs 295 k; on swift `pix_br` BRL 448 k vs 400 k and
`usd_acquirer` USD 547 k vs 375 k. Transfers: 77 vs 156 on stablecoin, 57 vs 70 on swift.

Alpha sweep (`intl_alpha_sweep.csv`): on stablecoin the shortfall rate is 0 % from alpha 0.95 (154 k);
on swift it is 4.8 % at 0.8 (466 k), 2.9 % at 0.95 (555 k) and 0 % only at 0.99 (637 k).

What it says:

- **Abroad, a fixed buffer sized like at home breaks on SWIFT**: 5 days of mean drain is short on
  52 % of partner-days, because two countries' holidays and the T+1 Tuesday lump (Friday-to-Monday
  card purchases settle together) stretch the time until a wire can land. It needs 6.6 days, a
  number nobody knows ex ante.
- **With stablecoin the forecast policy wins**: 43 % less capital than the fixed buffer at the same
  (0 %) shortfall rate, with half the transfers. Stablecoin plus forecast needs 204 k vs 455 k for
  the best SWIFT buffer (55 % less).
- **On SWIFT the forecast policy reaches 0 % shortfalls but holds more capital than the buffer tuned
  ex post on this same 52-day window.** Its Monte Carlo windows span up to five days of a lumpy T+1
  partner, and at alpha 0.99 it pays for that tail. Unlike the domestic wire run (19 % saved), the
  forecast does not beat a hindsight-tuned buffer here. That buffer is also matched on 104
  partner-days only, so its 0 % is a small sample.
- Not every partner takes stablecoin: when `usd_acquirer` stays on SWIFT, the gain on `pix_br`
  remains (USD 26 k vs 94 k), but the US partner dominates the total.

## Assumptions (guesses unless stated)

- Population shares: brazil_traveler 20 %, digital_subscriber 30 %, remitter 12 %, freelancer_usd
  10 %, occasional 28 %. Rates and amounts in `config/intl/archetypes.yaml` (Pix median BRL 70;
  subscriptions USD 10-20; remittance median USD 250 near payday; freelance payout median USD 600 at
  0.02/day). All guesses.
- Subscriptions are card-on-file: all confirmed (known amount and day), due day 1..28.
- FX parameters, elasticities (1.2 Pix, 0.4 USD cards), tourism lifts and their windows: guesses
  (winter school holidays vary by province; one window covers July 6-31).
- Rail fees (USD 30 + 30 bps SWIFT; USD 1 + 40 bps stablecoin), SWIFT cutoff 13:00 and credit 12:00:
  guesses. USD cost of capital 8 %: guess (Argentine corporate USD funding).
- Bank holidays: checked against FEBRABAN's 2026 list (as reported by the press) and the Federal
  Reserve's K.8 schedule; 31 December and Ash Wednesday are business days in Brazil.
- Amounts drift 0.3 %/month (US/BR inflation) instead of the domestic 2 %.
- Remittances settle with the card batch (T+1 at 17:00) because a partner has one settlement lag.
- All hours are Argentine time; US time zones are ignored (Brazil shares UTC-3).
